Definition

What is a Learning Space Integrator?

The short answer, what an LSI does, and how it differs from the firms schools already work with.

A Learning Space Integrator (LSI) is a specialized firm that helps school districts design, procure, and implement integrated learning environments, managing the work from educational vision through implementation so each space supports the district's learning goals.

Learning Space Integration is the practice of keeping learning intent intact across every stage of a school project: planning, design, purchasing, installation, and the months after move-in. An LSI is the partner responsible for that continuity.

What an LSI does

  • Assesses the district's current learning environments and educational vision
  • Designs physical spaces that match the district's teaching approach
  • Manages procurement of every component across multiple vendors
  • Coordinates implementation and teacher professional development
  • Supports the space after move-in so it keeps serving its learning goals

What an LSI is not

  • A general contractor or construction firm
  • A single-vendor furniture or technology dealer representing one product line
  • A general consultant with no specialized expertise in learning environments

How an LSI compares

Each of these partners plays an important role. The difference is where they start and what they are accountable for.

Integrated learning environments

Spaces where the physical environment, technology, and teaching approach work together to support the way a district wants students to learn.

Flexible

Spaces adapt to different learning modes: individual work, small-group collaboration, and large-group instruction.

Integrated technology

Technology is part of the learning process rather than bolted on after the fact.

Vision first

Design begins with the district's educational vision, not a product catalog.

School by school

Each school or building is planned, budgeted, and held accountable on its own.

Designed for every learner

Universal design addresses disabilities, sensory differences, neurodiversity, and varied learning needs from the start.

Measured the right way

Success is judged by space use, educator adoption, changes in teaching practice, and how students and staff experience the space. Standardized test scores are not a valid measure of a learning environment.

Questions districts ask about LSIs

What is a Learning Space Integrator?
A Learning Space Integrator (LSI) is a specialized firm that helps school districts design, procure, and implement integrated learning environments. It manages the process from educational vision through implementation, covering furniture, technology, and professional development, so each space supports the district's learning goals.
How is an LSI different from a furniture dealer?
A dealer typically starts from the products it represents and is accountable for supplying and installing them. An LSI starts from the district's learning goals, plans space, furniture, technology, and teaching practice together, manages sourcing across vendors with transparent disclosure of any vendor relationships, and stays involved after move-in.
When should a district bring in an LSI?
As early as possible, ideally during visioning and early design, before layouts and budgets are locked. Decisions about furniture and technology made at the end of a construction project are usually constrained by what was already decided.
How do districts hire an LSI?
Most districts use qualifications-based selection: either a Request for Qualifications (RFQ), where firms are ranked on qualifications and fees are negotiated with the top firm, or a Request for Proposals (RFP), where qualifications and price are scored together with published weights. Some cooperative purchasing programs may also offer a path. The free LSI Toolkit includes a process guide, RFQ template, scorecard, and interview guide.
How do you measure whether an LSI's work succeeded?
Look at space utilization, educator adoption, changes in instructional practice, and student and staff perception, measured before and after the project. Standardized test scores are not a valid measure of learning environment effectiveness.
