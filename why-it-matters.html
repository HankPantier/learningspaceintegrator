The problem

A new building is not the same as a better place to learn.

Most districts build or renovate a school once in a generation. The process that delivers it was designed to produce buildings on time and on budget. Nobody in it is responsible for making sure the finished spaces fit how students learn.

Four gaps in the usual process

Timing

Decisions happen at the wrong time

Furniture and technology are usually chosen at the end of the project, after layouts, power, and budgets are fixed. By then, what students need has to fit what is left.

Design handoffs

The building and the classroom are planned separately

Architects design the building and its rooms. How each room will be used for teaching is often handed off to a separate furniture allowance with little shared planning.

Teaching practice

Teaching practice is left out of the conversation

Teachers and instructional leaders are rarely part of design and purchasing decisions, then are expected to make new spaces work on the first day of school.

Procurement

Low bid decides what students sit in

When price is the main criterion, districts get the lowest-cost version of a product list, not the best outcome. Bidding rarely delivers the best long-term value for complex, vision-driven work.

One partner owns the thread from vision to activation

A Learning Space Integrator joins early, sets learning goals with the people who will use the spaces, and stays through installation and the months after move-in. Each decision is checked against the same question: does this support how our students learn?
